# 0140 — The first key is always lost, and a dock tune never survives the handoff

Status: Accepted

Supersedes ADR [0139](0139-opening-a-real-repeater.md)'s framing of the acceptance, and qualifies
the RF numbers in ADRs [0138](0138-baofeng-mode-proven-on-the-uvk5.md) and 0139.

## Context

The operator's question, and it is the right one:

> What good is any of this if I have to set the radio to a different channel each time?

None. A server that cannot select the repeater is not a repeater controller. ADR 0138 bought a
transmitter that works and paid for it with the only thing the product is for; that is acceptable as
a diagnostic step and not as a destination.

ADR 0139 planned to close this by having the operator dial K0PRA by hand and checking whether the
machine came back. **That acceptance is cancelled.** A repeater opens on carrier plus CTCSS, and both
are generated by the K5's own firmware from its front panel — so whether a thumb or the AIOC's DTR
line caused the key-up is irrelevant to whether the machine comes up. The verdict would have measured
the operator's antenna and tone setting, which he can already check with a bare handheld. It was a
carefully-built instrument pointed at the wrong quantity, which is this project's recurring mistake in
a new costume.

## The finding that stopped everything: the first key after idle is lost

Measured 2026-07-26. Same frequency, same request, same instrument, minutes apart:

| probe | result on 445.800 |
|---|---|
| `flaky_check` — 10 overs back to back | **10/10**, 1.19–1.28 s |
| first over after ~2 minutes idle | **0.00 s** |
| the next over, 4 s later | 1.24 s |

Isolated with `cold_key.py`, four trials, alternating which shape went first:

| | heard |
|---|---|
| **cold** 8 s continuous hold | **0/2** |
| **cold** 1.5 s over | **0/2** |
| warm 8 s hold | 2/2 (7.73, 7.72 s) |
| warm 1.5 s over | 2/2 (1.24, 1.18 s) |

**0/4 cold, 4/4 warm**, and an eight-second hold from cold radiates exactly as much as a short one:
nothing. So it is not a sleep window the key falls into, and length is not the variable.

Ruled out, each by measurement rather than argument:

- **Not a frequency change.** `carrier_hunt.py` holds PTT and sweeps the witness across all 48
  candidate frequencies in the kv4p's 400–480 MHz — every bench frequency, every UHF preset leg both
  ways, the usual defaults. On a silent over the carrier is **nowhere**. The radio does not move; it
  does not radiate.
- **Not USB power management.** The AIOC reports `power/control: on`,
  `autosuspend_delay_ms: -1000`, `runtime_suspended_time: 0` — autosuspend is disabled and the device
  has never suspended.
- **Not the witness.** A direct 20 Hz poll of the kv4p saw `busy` for 21 of 27 samples across an over
  that `busy_seconds` scored 0.00 s on a *different* run; and `watch_health.py` confirmed the poller
  alive with 719 samples inside a 1.5 s window and zero errors.
- **Not the server.** Every over returns 200, and `transmit()` blocks 1.51 s every single time —
  exactly the configured 1.0 s TX lead plus an 80 wpm CW "AE9S". The blocking contract and the audio
  path are correct while nothing goes on the air.
- **Not a wake-up pulse, at 0.5 s.** A throwaway 0.3 s key 0.5 s ahead of the real one: 0/3 treated
  against 0/3 interleaved cold controls.

### RETRACTED: the battery. The real cause is which VFO the radio transmits on.

An earlier revision of this ADR blamed a battery under its TX-inhibit threshold. **That was wrong.**
The battery reads **8.3 V, 99 %, and had been on the charger all day.** It was the second unmeasured
hardware excuse ranked ahead of a measurement in one session, and the rest of this section is what
measuring instead produced.

**First, radio-server was exonerated at the pin.** `pyserial`'s `.dtr` is write-only — reading it
returns what we assigned, not what the line is doing — so "the server keyed and the radio ignored
it" and "the server never keyed" had produced identical evidence all day. `GET /diagnostics/ptt-line`
now asks the kernel via `TIOCMGET`:

> **DTR HIGH on 16/16 samples, on 10 of 10 overs**, and correctly low when idle — while only 3/10
> produced a carrier.

The server keys perfectly, every time. The fault is downstream of the pin.

**Then the line choice was retested properly.** ADR 0138 picked `ptt_line = "dtr"` on *one* keying
per candidate. Interleaved (not block-ordered — this bench drifts, so block order ranks candidates by
*when they ran*): **dtr 5/10, rts 0/10, both 0/10.** DTR is right, now on ten samples instead of one.

**And DTR failed on exactly [2, 4, 6, 8, 10].** Strictly alternating. Reproduced in a fresh run at a
different spacing: passes on 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 and fails on 5, 7, 9, 11 — with attempt 2 catching a
*partial* 0.27 s carrier, the switch mid-flight.

**The hit rate is a function of the gap between keys:**

| gap | rate |
|---|---|
| 6 s | **9/10**, and 10/10 earlier the same day |
| 19 s | 5/12, strictly alternating |
| ~21 s (interleaved) | 5/10, strictly alternating |

Same radio, same code, minutes apart. **A broken transmitter does not care how long you wait between
keys; a periodically-switching VFO does.** That is aliasing against a periodic switch, and no loose
connector, cable, or battery can produce it.

**Mechanism.** `RADIO_SelectCurrentVfo` (`App/radio.c:715-721`) sets `gCurrentVfo` from `gRxVfo`
whenever dual watch is on, and dual watch alternates `gRxVfo` between the two VFOs on its own clock.
Whichever VFO the radio happens to be listening on when PTT asserts is the one it transmits from. So
roughly half of every station's overs go out on **the other VFO** — and the kv4p is SA818-**UHF**
(400–480 MHz), so if the other VFO sits on 2 m the witness physically cannot hear it. That is why
`carrier_hunt` swept 48 candidates and found the carrier "nowhere": it was looking in the only band
it can hear, and this ADR's own out-of-scope note already said 2 m is unreachable here.

**This is very likely the original bug.** An operator picks a repeater, keys once, hears nothing, and
concludes it does not work — with a 50 % chance of being right for the wrong reason. It also retires
ADR 0132/0134's "wrong-band PA bias" as a *symptom*: `Dock_ForceTx` sets the PA up from `gCurrentVfo`,
so the band mismatch alternates because the VFO alternates.

**The fix is already written.** F6's `0x0873` sets **both** VFOs — a precaution added on the reasoning
that dual watch makes the transmit frequency a matter of timing, before there was any evidence that
it was *the* fault. It now is the fix rather than a belt-and-braces detail.

### What this costs retroactively

**Every RF number in ADRs 0138 and 0139 is single- or two-shot.** `1926.8 / 1970.5`. `910–927`.
`1.21 s / RMS 3816.5`. All real, all measured while a coin-flip nobody knew about was live. They are
not thereby wrong — the radio demonstrably does transmit — but "Baofeng mode works" rested on samples
this bench can produce by luck, and that claim was over-stated.

`scripts/bench/trials.py` exists so it cannot happen again: **every RF claim from here is N-of-M with
N stated**, the full count runs even after a failure (how often it fails, and whether failures
cluster, is the diagnostic that found this), and `require_unanimous` keeps 1 = measured failure
distinct from 2 = inconclusive so an exit code still says which happened.

## A dock tune cannot survive the handoff — mechanism, not inference

`Dock_EnterFullControl` ends its loop with `RADIO_SetupRegisters(true)` (`App/app/uart.c:774`), and
that calls `BK4819_SetFrequency(gRxVfo->pRX->Frequency)` (`App/radio.c:807-809`). `0x0871` **retunes
the chip from the radio's own VFO on the way out.** Every dock write — `0x38/0x39` frequency,
`0x51/0x07` CTCSS, `0x43` bandwidth — is transient by construction: `0x0870` backs the registers up,
`0x0871` puts them back.

That also explains the original bug without inventing a new hypothesis. The host owns the synthesiser;
the firmware owns the PA and reads `gCurrentVfo` (`uart.c:729-732`). They are different objects. For
four cycles the dock has been driving a transmitter whose power stage was set up for somewhere else,
which is why 37 presets could be applied, read back correctly, and never key a machine.

The measurement that would confirm this end-to-end **did not complete**: three attempts each died on
their own positive control, because the cold-key fault killed the baseline. The instrument refusing to
answer is the instrument working. It is recorded here as unmeasured, and the cold-key fault is why.

## Decision — set the VFO, do not write registers

New fork opcode, `kbennett2000/uv-k1-k5v3-firmware-custom` branch `f6-dock-set-vfo`.

`0x0873` hands the radio a whole channel — rx frequency, offset and direction, CTCSS in tenths of a
Hz, bandwidth, power — writes it into `gEeprom.VfoInfo`, and then runs the firmware's **own** chain:
`RADIO_ApplyOffset` (`radio.c:703`) for the split, `RADIO_ConfigureSquelchAndOutputPower`
(`radio.c:493`) for the per-band PA calibration that lives in flash the host cannot read and must not
invent (the mistake ADR 0128 removed), then `RADIO_SetupRegisters`. Afterwards `0x0871` stops being
something to survive and becomes the thing that applies the setting: the radio is genuinely on the
channel, screen and all, as if a thumb had dialled it.

Four choices worth defending:

1. **`0x0873`, not `0x0872`.** `0x0872` is the stock `CMD_0872_t` (SetModulation) — defined in
   `uart.c` and carried in our own `DockCommand` enum, merely never dispatched. Reusing it would
   redefine a documented command on this radio and silently reinterpret anyone else's frame.
2. **It dispatches with `0x0850`/`0x0851`/`0x0871`, never with `0x0870`.** Entering the full-control
   loop to tune would starve the main loop — the same loop that samples the PTT pin — which is the
   output being set up.
3. **Both VFOs are written.** `gCurrentVfo` follows dual watch (`RADIO_SelectCurrentVfo`), so setting
   one leaves which frequency we transmit on up to a timer.
4. **Malformed and out-of-range frames are refused, never clamped**, on both sides. A bad direction
   byte read as "simplex" transmits on the repeater's *output* — on top of the machine and whoever it
   is repeating. CTCSS is matched against the radio's actual table, not a range: 123.4 Hz is inside
   [67.0, 254.1] and is not a tone the radio has, and the firmware would drop it and send no tone,
   which from the operating position is indistinguishable from a repeater that will not open.

The host-side validation is duplicated deliberately: the firmware has no reply channel, so it refuses
in silence, and a caller that got the encoding wrong would otherwise see success and a radio that
never moved.

## Also found: the live backend switch segfaults the server

`POST /radio/select {"backend": "uvk5"}` from `baofeng` **crashed the server** — status 139 (SIGSEGV),
49 s into the rebuild. `close()` never ran, so `0x0871` was never sent. ADR 0139 recorded this path as
safe on the strength of the *other* direction (`uvk5` → `baofeng`). It is reachable from the web UI.
Not yet fixed; recorded here so it is not rediscovered.

## What this does not claim

- **The second frequency has never been read.** The alternation, its dependence on key spacing,
  and the mechanism in `RADIO_SelectCurrentVfo` are all measured or in source, but nothing here
  has observed the *other* VFO directly: the bench has no VHF receiver, and the firmware that
  could report a VFO is not flashed. Confirmation is a 2 m receiver, or F6 plus a commanded
  channel. Until then the alternating VFO is an inference from a deterministic timing signature —
  a strong one, but named as an inference.
- **The firmware has never met a radio.** Host tests pass (48 checks, 0 failures) and the image builds
  clean (FLASH 57 %, RAM 64 %), but nothing here has been flashed. Every claim about `0x0873` is a
  claim about source and host tests.
- **No repeater has been opened**, and absolute RF power is still unmeasured — ADR 0137's open gap,
  untouched.
- **Nothing on 2 m.** The kv4p is SA818-UHF (400–480 MHz), so the 15 VHF repeaters, and any carrier
  that lands there, remain outside every instrument on this bench.
